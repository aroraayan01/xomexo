We buy the way we'd want to be bought from

Outright. In advance. At a price the maker names. Everything else about Xomexo follows from those three rules.

2016 – today

It started with a bad invoice

Our founder was sourcing terracotta for a hotel fit-out and asked to see what the potter had actually been paid. It was eleven percent of the invoice. The agent in between had done nothing but move boxes.

Xomexo began as an attempt to remove that agent. We drive to the cluster, sit with the family, agree a price, pay half up front and the rest on collection. There is no third party in the chain and no consignment — if a batch does not sell, that is our problem, not the maker's.

It caps how fast we can grow. Twelve products is not a lot after ten years. But the twelve are honest.

How a piece gets listed

Four steps, about nine months

Slower than it sounds. Most of it is spent deciding whether we should be selling the thing at all.

01

We visit

Someone from the team spends two to three days at the workshop. We watch the whole process, meet whoever is doing the work, and ask what they are currently paid.

02

They price it

The artisan sets the number, not us. We ask for a breakdown — material, hours, firing, wastage — and we write it down. Then we work out whether we can sell at a margin on top.

03

A trial batch

Six to ten pieces, paid for in full, shipped to real customers at cost. If they arrive broken or nobody wants them, we say so and stop there.

04

Standing order

A signed twelve-month agreement with a minimum quantity, 50% advance, and a fixed price for the term. It is the advance that matters most — it means the family is not borrowing to buy clay.

·

What we refuse

Anything using ivory, shahtoosh, protected timber or chrome tanning. Anything a child worked on. Anything a workshop cannot let us photograph freely.

·

What we get wrong

Lead times, mostly. Handmade batches slip and we have been slow to admit it on product pages. We now publish the real dispatch window, not the optimistic one.

Packing

Nothing plastic goes in the box

Rice husk instead of foam peanuts, corrugated kraft instead of bubble wrap, paper tape instead of PVC. It costs us about ₹40 more per parcel and adds a step to packing day.

The trade-off is a slightly higher breakage rate on ceramics — around 1.4% against an industry norm near 0.9%. We replace breakages free and count it as the price of the choice, rather than quietly switching back to foam.
